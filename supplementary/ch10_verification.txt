Dark-red corrections and necessary qualifications:
- 10.1.2(b): finite must mean bounded at both infinities; pointwise finite
  exponential homogeneous solutions would otherwise destroy uniqueness.
- 10.1.5: distinguish D=x^2 d^2+x d+k^2 x^2-1 from the manual's L=D/x.
  The symmetric manual kernel obeys L G_L=delta; D G_L=t delta. The printed
  operator's ordinary-source kernel is G_D=G_L/t. Positive roots J_1(k)=0
  are resonant; k=0 is separately nonresonant. Endpoint/source restrictions
  and the sign choice for real k are explicit.
- 10.1.7: the manual uses an integrating-factor kernel and a compensating
  source weight. The direct-source kernel is stated, including k=0 and
  the removable k=1 singularity of the response formula.
- 10.1.8: textbook Eq.(10.28), p.454, omits k from its exponent. The
  exercise and Table 10.1 have the correct ik|x-x'|. The outgoing time
  convention is explicit.
- 10.1.9: decay requires the positive root |k|; no two-sided decaying
  free inverse exists for k=0 in one dimension.
- 10.1.12: the unit-weight self-adjoint discussion requires real
  coefficients; an integrating factor gives a weighted formulation when
  a real first-derivative coefficient is nonzero.
- 10.1.13: the decaying free kernel needs Re k>0 and a regular origin
  branch. The manual's last sentence incorrectly calls the printed kernel
  minus the Green function; its derivative jump is +1. The zero-k free
  limit fails the requested decay boundary condition.
- 10.2.1: the fundamental +d^2 kernel is minus the -d^2 kernel used in
  Example 10.1.1; the operator sign is retained throughout.
- 10.2.2: common homogeneous Dirichlet domains, vanishing boundary forms
  at infinity where relevant, and the distinction between symmetric and
  abstract self-adjoint operators are made explicit.
- 10.2.3: the source's shrinking-region integral equal to -1 is false.
  The question now explicitly integrates over a shrinking ball and gives
  the proved local limit 0. The whole-space modified-kernel integral is
  separately -1 and cannot be substituted for that local limit.
- 10.2.5: the customary standing-wave kernel is the half sum of outgoing
  and incoming kernels. Standing-wave behavior alone leaves a smooth
  real homogeneous addition C sin(kr)/r and does not imply uniqueness.
- 10.2.6: positive decay root; the k=0 three-dimensional Laplace limit.
- 10.2.7: a>0 is needed for exponential screening and zero net charge;
  the a=0 potential has point charge Z and no screening cloud.
